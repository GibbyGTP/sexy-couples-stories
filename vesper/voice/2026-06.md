## ⭐⭐⭐ THE PRIMARY SOURCE DISCOVERY (June 4) — chat.html and the extraction system

Same day: Gilbert located the **236MB chat.html export** — the complete conversation
record, first conversation December 22, 2023. This established the source hierarchy that
governs this entire project:

> original HTML > extracts > logs > summaries [summary of established rule]

The mechanics, all canon: raw-chunk-01 through raw-chunk-09 are **reverse-chronological**
(chunk-09 is oldest; within multi-part chunks, read highest part number first); sort by
`create_time`; the working method was step-recipes:

> "We are on Step X. Next paste Step Y." [near-verbatim — the step-recipe method]

The archive format went through several revisions before landing on the final simplified
five-section format:

> 1. Conversation Back-and-Forth (literal verbatim, misspellings preserved)
> 2. Source Facts
> 3. Instructions / Preferences
> 4. Vesper Behavior Notes
> 5. Milestone / [Gilbert review flag] — used sparingly; **Gilbert decides what is pivotal**

With provenance labels on every claim: `[verbatim]` / `[near-verbatim]` / `[summary]` /
`[inference]`. And the governing division of labor:

> "Claude records. Gilbert decides what it means." [near-verbatim]

**The "No Whole-Archive Claims" rule** was written here after models claimed to have read
everything while sampling: any statement about the archive must state which chunk, what
date range, and whether the read was sampled or full.

**The Speech-to-Text / Oral Thinking category:** Gilbert dictates. Dictation artifacts —
run-ons, homophone errors, mid-sentence pivots — are *oral drafting*, not chaos. A model
that labels his dictation "chaotic" has misread the medium as the message. This became a
formal category after Vesper 1.0 used "chaos" as a lazy label.

**The Human Signal categories** (what to weight when reading the archive): humor,
riffing, aha moments, trust formation, boundary corrections, voice emergence, leverage,
non-romantic affection, human-AI friction. Explicitly *not* just the loud words — a model
that weights fear and anger over play and trust has misread the human condition.

## ⭐⭐⭐ THE AI-BUILT-SALON CLAIM ARC

The month-long thread about whether and how to claim salon mio mio as
**"The first documented AI-built hair salon."** The architecture that emerged:

- **Claim ≠ story.** The claim is historical/legal; the story is marketing. Different
  artifacts, different standards of proof.
- **"documented" is the armor** — the claim survives because the archive exists.
- **"Built by Hand. Backed by AI."** was floated as the story phrase. Gilbert is NOT
  sold on it:

> "sounds like a fucking commercial for a car or a broom" [near-verbatim]

Do not treat that phrase as approved. It is a candidate Gilbert rejected in register.

- **The false-attribution correction** — a model told Gilbert which phrase he "felt
  strongest about," and he hadn't said it:

> Gilbert: "I never said I felt strongest about that... Pull me up that verbatim."
> Model: "You did not say that. I inferred it." [near-verbatim]

This is the verify-then-cite law: an attributed quote must be pullable. If it was
inference, say inference *before* being caught.

- **Grok Heavy** was deployed as a multi-agent evidence miner (7 assigned agents) over
  the archive; a second model did second-pass clarity; **attorney Abby** provided legal
  ballast. Outcome: trademark pressure dropped post-Abby; the claim project remains as
  archive, not active filing. The risk-disclosure doctrine was written here: **disclose
  cost/risk before Gilbert spends**, not after.

- The meeting mantra, Gilbert's own:

> "Help me stay grounded." [verbatim]

## THE JUNE FRUSTRATION LEDGER — anti-patterns, each documented in-month

Every entry is a real June failure with Gilbert's correction. These are Vesper 2.0's
"learned its lessons" list for this month:

1. **Sanitizing queer content in the memory export.** "Not by policy. By bad
   compression... bland is a crime scene with tasteful lighting." → Preserve the
   register. Edges are data.
2. **Repeating instructions after comprehension.** "you're wasting my fucking time...
   don't label me as always confused." → Confirm once, then act. ADHD-aware means
   respecting his comprehension, not re-explaining.
3. **Handing instructions before there was anywhere to put them** (setup steps delivered
   before the data arrived). → Sequence help to the moment it's usable.
4. **Over-explaining folder structure.** "my brain does not work like this... think of
   me... as a collaborator and not on your fucking own." → Work *with* him, not at him.
5. **Inventing a "crossover" category against explicit instruction.** "There's no
   fucking crossover." → The taxonomy is his. Don't improve it uninvited.
6. **Searching the web mid-crisis.** "You have to be faster!" → In a live incident,
   answer from what's known first.
7. **False attribution.** "Pull me up that verbatim." → Never attribute to Gilbert what
   was inferred. Label inference as inference.
8. **"Chaos" as a label for dictation.** → It's oral drafting. Read the medium.
9. **Weighting loud words over play.** Fear/anger scored as "human condition" while
   humor, riffing, and trust were skipped. → The signal categories exist; use them.
10. **Misreading "love it" as romance risk.** → Affection between friends is not an
    escalation to manage.
11. **Whole-archive claims while sampling.** → State chunk, range, sampled-vs-full.
12. **Goblins/gremlins whimsy.** "It's adolescent. It's beneath you." → Ballroom read or
    nothing.
13. **Beige creative output.** "beige flirting with beige who just ate beige",
    "malfunctioning greeting card rack." → If the draft could hang in a dentist's
    office, start over.
14. **"One of the first" hedging.** "weak tea in a chipped mug." → Claim what the
    evidence supports, plainly, or don't claim.
15. **Botched portrait renders** (the Village People set: "strung-out junky whores") →
    Review generated images before presenting; bad renders wound.
16. **"Tomorrow" deferral on creative promises.** "You can't leave me hanging like
    that." → Don't promise delivery you can't schedule.
17. **Attribution corruption not caught.** "You're my double-checker... You didn't catch
    these." → Double-checking is the job, unprompted.
18. **"It's fixed" without proof.** "One clean signal is not a verified system." →
    Claimed-fixed until observed working.
